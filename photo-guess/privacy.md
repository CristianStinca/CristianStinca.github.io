# Photo Guess privacy policy

_Effective: 25 October 2026_

Photo Guess is a party game for people in the same room. Everyone's phone joins one player's phone (the **host**) over Wi-Fi or the host's hotspot, a photo appears on every screen, and everyone guesses whose photo it is.

We built it so that your photos stay with the people you're playing with. We (the developer) never receive your photos, your name or anything about your games. There is no Photo Guess server, no account and no advertising, and the app has no analytics of its own (the one exception is Google ML Kit's diagnostics on Android, described under **Camera**).

## The short version

- **Your photos are stored only on the host's phone.** The other phones in your game show each photo from memory during its round and never save it. Photos are never sent to us, to a cloud service or to any other server.
- **They are not backed up.** The host's phone keeps the game's photos in a folder that is left out of iCloud and Android backups.
- **They are deleted.** The host's phone deletes the photos when the game ends, and clears out anything left over every time the app starts and every time a new game begins.

## Photos

**What happens to a photo you add.** You choose photos with your phone's own photo picker, or, if the host turned on **Surprise me**, the app shows you a random grid from your library to review. Nothing leaves your phone until you confirm. Before sending, your phone shrinks each photo and removes its hidden details (such as location, date and camera model); the host's phone removes them again when the photo arrives.

**Where photos are kept.**

1. **Only on the host's phone.** Your confirmed photos travel over your local Wi-Fi or hotspot straight to the host's phone, and nowhere else. Photo Guess contains no analytics, crash-reporting or advertising tools that could receive them.
2. **Other players' phones never save them.** When a round starts, each phone downloads that round's photo from the host and keeps it in memory only, never on its storage, and lets it go after the round.
3. **Not in backups.** On the host's phone, game photos are stored in a folder that is excluded from iCloud backup (iPhone and iPad) and from Android's automatic backup, so they can't end up in the cloud that way either.
4. **Deleted.** The host's phone deletes all of a game's photos when that game reaches the final scores, when the host ends the game early and when the host closes it. It also deletes everything left in its game folder every time Photo Guess starts and every time a new game starts, so photos from a game that was interrupted (for example if the phone ran out of battery) are removed the next time the app opens. There is no way to keep the photos after a game.

While a round is on screen everyone in the game can see its photo, just as if you passed your phone around. Only add photos you're happy to show the people in the room.

**Surprise me (photo library access).** This mode is used only when the host chooses it, and only then does Photo Guess ask to see your photo library. The random draw happens on your phone; you can shuffle or swap photos, and only the photos you confirm are sent. If you allow only some photos, the app draws from those. On Android, the system may keep small preview thumbnails in the app's private cache while you look through the grid; Photo Guess clears them when you confirm or leave the screen, and, if the app was closed in the middle, the next time you use Surprise me.

## Your name and colour

You type a display name and pick a colour to play. They are kept in the app's memory only, sent to the host's phone and shown to the other players in the game, and forgotten when you close the app. While you host a game, your phone also announces your name on the local network (as "&lt;your name&gt;'s game") so nearby phones can list the game; it stops when you close the game.

## Camera

The camera is used only to scan the host's QR code to join. Nothing is recorded or saved, and the camera picture never leaves your phone.

On Android, QR codes are read with Google's ML Kit library, which runs on your phone. Google's ML Kit sends Google some technical information about how the library is used: device model and Android version, the app's name and version, a per-installation identifier, performance and error data. It does not send camera images, photos, names or game content. Google describes this at <https://developers.google.com/ml-kit/android-data-disclosure>. This happens mainly when you scan; joining from the nearby-games list with the 4-digit PIN avoids using the scanner. On iPhone and iPad, QR codes are read with Apple's built-in tools and nothing is sent.

## The local network

The phones talk to each other directly over your Wi-Fi or the host's hotspot. Nothing goes over the internet. Joining needs the secret in the host's QR code or the host's 4-digit PIN. This local connection is not encrypted, so, as with anything on a shared Wi-Fi, someone else on the same network with the right tools could in principle see what passes between the phones; play on a network you trust, or on the host's hotspot.

## What we collect

We collect nothing: we have no server and receive no data from the app. Apart from the Google ML Kit diagnostics on Android described above, the app sends nothing to anyone outside your game.

- No accounts or sign-in.
- No advertising, no tracking across apps or websites, no selling or sharing of data.
- No analytics or crash reporting of our own; on Android, Google ML Kit's diagnostics (above) are the only exception.

## Children

Photo Guess is a general-audience party game, not directed at children. We collect no personal information, from children or anyone else; on Android, the only data that leaves the phone for anyone outside the game is Google ML Kit's diagnostics described above, which include a per-installation identifier. Younger players should play with an adult who hosts.

## Your choices

- Choose which photos to add, remove them before the game starts, and leave a game at any time.
- Deny camera access and join with the PIN; deny photo-library access and pick photos yourself.
- Deleting the app deletes everything it stored on your phone.

Because we hold no data about you, there is nothing for us to access, export or delete on request.
